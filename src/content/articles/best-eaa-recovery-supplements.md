faqs:
  - q: "EAAs or BCAAs — which should I choose?"
    a: "For muscle protein synthesis, EAAs are the better bet because your body needs all nine essential amino acids, not just the three branched-chain ones. BCAAs still work for flavoring water and light recovery support, but a full EAA is more complete. If you already eat plenty of protein, either is optional rather than essential."
  - q: "Do I even need amino acid supplements?"
    a: "If your daily protein intake is already adequate — roughly 0.7–1 g per pound of body weight — whole-food protein covers your amino needs and supplements add little. Aminos are most useful for fasted training, very high-volume blocks, endurance sessions or days when protein runs low."
  - q: "When should I take EAAs or BCAAs?"
    a: "The most common windows are during (intra-workout) or right after training, though timing matters less than total daily protein. Sipping aminos during longer or fasted sessions can help with hydration and reduce that late-workout fatigue for some people."
  - q: "Are amino acid supplements safe?"
    a: "For healthy adults, EAAs and BCAAs are generally well tolerated at label doses. If you have kidney or liver conditions, are pregnant, or take medication, check with your doctor first, since higher protein and amino intakes can matter in those cases."
