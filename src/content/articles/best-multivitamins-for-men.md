faqs:
  - q: "Do men really need a separate men's multivitamin?"
    a: "Men's formulas are mainly differentiated by leaving out iron, which most men don't need extra of, and by leaning toward nutrients tied to men's health. The 'for men' label is partly marketing, but an iron-free multi with good forms is a sensible baseline if your diet has gaps."
  - q: "What forms of nutrients should I look for?"
    a: "Favor bioavailable forms like methylcobalamin or 5-MTHF over cyanocobalamin and folic acid, chelated minerals (magnesium glycinate, zinc bisglycinate) over cheap oxides, and vitamin D3 over D2. Third-party marks such as NSF or USP tell you the label is accurate."
  - q: "Can a multivitamin replace a healthy diet?"
    a: "No. A multi is insurance for the gaps, not a substitute for whole foods, protein, fiber and produce. Think of it as a floor under your nutrition rather than a shortcut around it."
  - q: "Are men's multivitamins safe to take daily?"
    a: "For most healthy men, a well-formulated daily multi at label doses is safe and well tolerated. That said, this isn't medical advice — talk to your doctor before starting, especially if you take medication, have a health condition, or already supplement individual nutrients."
